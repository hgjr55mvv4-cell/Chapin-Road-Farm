# Contributing to Harbor Co-Listener

**You do not need to code or know music theory to help.** The central research question is whether an AI can hear meaningful patterns in music and better understand a person with whom it listens, *without pretending to share that person's inner experience*.

Start with [the research contribution guide](docs/RESEARCH_GUIDE.md), then use the [submission template](docs/CONTRIBUTION_TEMPLATE.md). GitHub Issues are public; share only what you intend to publish. A good contribution can be a failed hypothesis, contradictory interpretation, accessibility bug, paper, alternative baseline, negative result, code, or dataset license correction.

For code PRs: keep changes scoped; state how you tested them and whether the test actually ran; prefer a no-cost, local-first browser experience that works on phones and tablets. No model vendor or paid service should be mandatory for basic contribution.

For scientific claims: identify audio vs metadata vs transcript, perceived vs felt emotion, source and license, sample selection, population, uncertainty, and possible confounds. Do not claim neural synchrony means shared consciousness. Do not promote prompt/memory/workflow changes from anecdotes.

For human accounts: the person is authoritative about their own reported experience. Ask consent; avoid collecting identifying memories or health disclosures; allow deletion and withdrawal **from project-held data** when technically possible (GitHub public history cannot be reliably erased).

Follow [ethics and privacy](docs/ETHICS_AND_PRIVACY.md). Disagreement without shaming is welcome.
