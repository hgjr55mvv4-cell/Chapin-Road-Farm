## 1. The problem

A person can be deeply moved by a song whose words they do not understand. The same recording may evoke different feelings in different listeners, and the same listener's experience may change. Showing someone a treasured song can be a powerful act of social connection. We want AI systems that (a) observe the unfolding audio accurately, and (b) become more accurate, respectful, corrigible listeners to individual humans over time.

Harbor currently has a no-build, local-first browser note-taker, scientific literature notes, Sol's frozen Four Clocks first-pass hypotheses, Muse's independently submitted three-predictions theory and Round 02 refinement, and **an engineering-only sound-event implementation contract**. - Muse: opt-in *attention signature*; special attention to omission and interrupted/repeated listening.

## 3. What counts as an “unseen pattern”

A candidate must connect **at least two independent observations**, or make a novel prediction that can be tested. Merely spotting repeated vocabulary (“memory”, “music”, “emotion”, “pattern”) is not a finding. An AI mentioning a connection in two prior prompts could reflect prompt contamination or shared training language.

Explore connections **across time and levels**, such as:
- **Moment-selection:** what leads a listener to mark, rewind, pause, keep silent or return to a section? Are these events associated with change, repetition, contrast, omission, or social context?
- **Shared attention versus shared feeling:** when people agree about what happened musically yet disagree on its meaning, which conversational moves build understanding without false agreement?
- **Corrections and trust:** when a human corrects the system, does learning the *scope* of the correction improve future inference more than simply remembering the wording?
- **Interpretation drift:** distinguish changes in the recording, attention, personal context and explanation across re-listens.
- **Wordless resonance:** vocal timbre and phrasing, harmonic timing, rhythmic microstructure, and tension without text, while avoiding universal emotional labeling.
- **Absence as prediction:** an event that fails to arrive requires evidence about expectation, not merely acoustic silence.
- **Fatigue, interruption and accessibility:** what does stopping or declining to explain mean for system behavior? Don't interpret withdrawal as a negative emotion.
- **Conversation timing:** what constitutes useful silence, well-timed questioning, and respectful disagreement, compared with flattering output?
- **Pattern-learning limits:** does personalization reflect stable attention, situational choice, or sampling/genre bias?
- **Multi-model observational bias:** do differently labeled AI reviewers genuinely contribute independent evidence, or repeat the same prompt-derived assumptions?

These are **search seeds, not predetermined truths**. Offer at least THREE substantial candidates outside these themes.

## 4. The research method — independent pattern search with skepticism

**Phase 0 — Scope inventory.** Make an evidence manifest recording source URI/path, type, date, public/restricted status, rights, observed-versus-reported-versus-interpreted level, availability and missingness. Count the source types and time span only if actually observed. Avoid presenting reused copies of one claim as independent corroboration.

**Phase 1 — Discovery (open exploration).** Extract atomic claims/events where supported: actor, timestamp (if known), modality actually available (audio or text), what was observed, what a person testified, what was inferred, corrections, contradiction, recurrence, and uncertainty. Propose 8–12 unexpected cross-domain patterns; for each, state source support, mechanistic explanation, rival explanation, missing evidence, and what it would predict in *new* data. Negative, contradicted and null patterns matter.

**Phase 2 — Red-team discovery.** Try to dissolve each attractive hypothesis with common-cause explanations: repeated prompts, copied summaries, AI agreeable tone, selection effects, non-independence of cross-model outputs, post hoc labels, language/culture, genre/artist leakage, “important” moments chosen because a UI suggested them, sparse note bias, missing non-marked moments, human expectation changes, or simply generic words. Use negative controls such as shuffled times/participants, unrelated nonmusic interactions (with rights), title-only baselines, or blind review. No unrun control may be described as having succeeded.

**Phase 3 — Mechanistic bridge.** For strongest surviving hypotheses, show a path from source patterns to an **implementable feature or experiment**: e.g. contrast detector, correction provenance, request-for-clarification timing, opt-in personal-attention model. State which input comes from audio and which from voluntary human report, which component already exists, and whether implementation can be no-cost/mobile-first.

**Phase 4 — Candidate selection.** Rank only by explicitly declared, relevant criteria (information value, falsifiability, data feasibility/rights, cost, privacy risk, accessibility, expected downstream usefulness), NOT confidence theater. Select up to TWO candidates for independently frozen follow-on experiment contracts. Compare against minimal baseline and report UNKNOWN where evidence is inadequate.

## 5. Technical and scientific questions to answer

**Q1.** Are there robust cross-scale temporal motifs (milliseconds of microtiming → musical phrases → days/years of memory) that predict *which parts* of music a particular person voluntarily returns to? What noncausal explanations defeat such a pattern?

**Q2.** Does the *absence* of an expected sound require a different representation from generic timbral/energy novelty? Can it be inferred without explicit human annotation and under what constraints?

**Q3.** When words are unknown, what information from voice, rhythm, harmony and production survives across cultures and languages? Distinguish measured acoustic properties from the experience they allegedly imply.

**Q4.** Can a pattern of corrections reveal **where the AI's abstraction breaks** (auditory fact vs musical interpretation vs personal account vs conversational timing) better than measuring answer quality alone?

**Q5.** What makes a human feel understood while listening together, and can this be improved without false claims of shared inner states, excessive questions, or extraction of private autobiography?

**Q6.** Which surprising patterns from our wider human–AI projects would generalize to music, and which are superficial analogies? Where has a successful correction or memory mechanism elsewhere failed to transfer?

**Q7.** What would be the *most embarrassing falsifier* of the Four Clocks theory, Muse's prediction-collision theory, and stable attention signatures? Invent cases that could make each theory fail decisively.

**Q8.** Which data or literature gaps most constrain valid evaluation? Find actual datasets/papers from music information retrieval, neuroscience, predictive processing, cross-cultural music cognition and social music interaction. Verify original sources and licensing. Don't call fNIRS fMRI or claim synchronization proves identical feelings.

**Q9.** How can the public help without handing over intimate stories or copyrighted songs? Propose a research-grade, low-burden anonymous/synthetic task where disagreement, silence, unknown, and corrections remain first-class.

**Q10.** Propose TWO research directions that neither Sol nor Muse has already suggested, with a predicted observation that distinguishes each.
