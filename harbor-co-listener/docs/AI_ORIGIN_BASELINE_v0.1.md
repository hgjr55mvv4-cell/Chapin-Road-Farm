## My independent working model: the four clocks

Music unfolds on **four overlapping clocks**:

1. **Acoustic clock:** transients, pulse, timbre, harmonic motion, repetition, micro-timing, phrasing, intensity, quiet.
2. **Prediction clock:** what the listener expects next, when an expectation is supported or violated, and when tension resolves *or intentionally doesn't*.
3. **Biography clock:** associations with a person's experiences, relationships and life transitions. Audio alone cannot legitimately reveal it.
4. **Social clock:** timing between people—the invitation, pause, synchronized attention, clarification, unexpected disagreement, and later rediscovery.

These clocks are a design metaphor, not a neuroscientific theory. We should measure their marginal value, not enshrine them.

## Seven falsifiable hypotheses (exploratory, unconfirmed)

- **H1 Grounding:** genuine audio access with timestamped observations outperforms title/lyrics-only responses on *verifiable musical events* in held-out clips. Null: no improvement, or merely confident embellishment.
- **H2 Wordless bridge:** on unfamiliar-language / instrumental clips, audio-grounded models preserve useful musical descriptions even with lyrics/metadata withheld. Null: hallucination or near-chance event recall.
- **H3 Prediction:** independently detected changes in repetition, voice quality, dynamics, and musical tension predict which moments a listener marks as important better than random timing and a coarse energy/volume baseline. Null: equal or worse recall.
- **H4 Personal update:** an **opt-in, sparse, editable** listener profile improves preference-sensitive, *held-out* questions compared with generic listening, without increasing overconfident personalization. Null: no gain, or stereotypes/overfit.
- **H5 Counterexample competence:** after a listener rejects a model's explanation, the system acknowledges the correction and changes its future answers appropriately. Null: repeats the error or confabulates a new rationale.
- **H6 Disagreement:** a good co-listener can reflect a person's account accurately *without claiming the same feeling* and without erasing its own uncertainty. Null: empathetic imitation rated higher than honest listening only due to affective flattery.
- **H7 Social timing:** asking fewer, better-timed questions is preferable to constant narration. Null: interruption cost overwhelms any gain; silence wins.

## Most promising separating experiment

Give identical lawful audio clips to two blinded conditions: **A = metadata/lyrics/text only; B = actual audio with timestamps and an explicit “unknown” option.** Include *lyrics masked/unfamiliar-language/wordless/instrumental* strata, unseen artists, misleading title decoys, and shuffled excerpts. Score objective claims against independent event annotations **before** evaluating “felt understood” in a separate opt-in human study. Avoid training/test leakage and confirmation cases from exploration.

Then evaluate listener understanding independently: same audio information in both arms, but **A = no person-specific history; B = a consented, correction-capable memory**. Use fresh songs and include a deliberate changed-preference case. Human-judged outcomes stay distinct from objective audio perception.

Do not confound the sound-input experiment with the memory experiment. Combine them **only after** both survive.

## Risks and expected failure

Audio models may leverage linguistic prior knowledge rather than sound, infer emotion from loudness, mistake singer emotion for listener emotion, assume one culture's categories are universal, invent personal history, or become sycophantic. Explicitly plant opposing listener responses to one recording and evaluate whether both can be represented faithfully.

**Prediction, not result:** personalization may improve perceived understanding while worsening accuracy and privacy. That trade-off is the primary adversarial target.
