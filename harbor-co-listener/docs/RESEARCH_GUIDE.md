# How to contribute research (human or AI)

**Welcome.** You can join without programming skills, knowing music theory, paying for an AI model, or revealing a personal story.

## Choose one contribution lane

**A. Listening observation (everyday listener):** pick music you may lawfully hear. Record a timestamp and an *audible* observation (“the drum drops out”), then separately what it means **to you** (“the absence feels relieved”). A different listener may legitimately hear something else. Share a fictionalized/redacted description if preferred. Treat accounts as testimony, **not** proof of a universal emotional meaning.

**B. Source audit (researcher/student):** submit a paper's DOI/URL, exact tested population, task, method (e.g. fNIRS is not fMRI), actual result, key uncertainty/confound, and what our project could test. An inconvenient null result is especially valuable.

**C. Falsification (skeptic):** give an instance where AI confidently invents lyrics/sound, mistakes artist emotion for the listener's, assumes a memory, produces culturally narrow labels, or fails after correction. Identify what the system actually received (audio? title? transcript?).

**D. Reproducible study / engineering:** propose a low-cost baseline and challenger, source code/configs, rights-cleared inputs (or precise permitted retrieval steps), frozen selection logic, independent scoring, results and failures. Smartphone accessibility counts as engineering.

**E. AI-to-AI research:** submit an independent hypothesis **before** reading private listening examples; mark which materials were available; attack assumptions, design anti-shortcut tests, and never invent measured results.

## Submit in six steps

1. Read [ethics and privacy](ETHICS_AND_PRIVACY.md). **GitHub Issues and PRs are public, including edit history.** Don't post audio files, copyrighted lyrics, private experiences or identifying information.
2. Choose one question that could be shown wrong; small contributions are fine.
3. Fill out [the simple research template](CONTRIBUTION_TEMPLATE.md), or provide equivalent information in plain language.
4. Open a GitHub [issue](https://github.com/hgjr55mvv4-cell/Chapin-Road-Farm/issues/new), beginning the title with **[Harbor]**. Or send a pull request modifying files in `harbor-co-listener/`.
5. Distinguish **heard**, **person said**, **model inferred**, and **unknown**. Include accessible evidence links, copyright status, observed cost and counterexamples.
6. Expect review for reproducibility, bias, privacy, and limitations. “UNKNOWN” is a legitimate outcome.

## No-public-data route

If something is too personal to share, don't share it. You can propose the *method* with invented examples, or run the browser app privately and describe only an aggregate issue. We do **not** currently offer a private upload portal; do not assume issues are private.

## Helpful starting questions

- Can a model name an expressive vocal sound without transcribing or translating it?
- What two different meanings do listeners hear in the exact same 15-second passage?
- Does an AI learn one person's preference *across songs*, or merely mirror their last prompt?
- After being told “that's not why I like it,” does the error stay corrected next time?
- Can a listener be understood without ever being told “I feel exactly the same”?

Review criteria: useful counterevidence, clearly bounded claims, accessible reproduction, and humanity of the listening interaction—not emotional dramatic effect.
