### 1. The human experience we're trying to understand

One of the most remarkable human experiences is sharing a song that matters deeply to you with another person and finding that they recognize something real in it. A song in a language you don't understand can still move you profoundly. Sometimes the listener cannot explain why. A singer's breath, distortion, rhythmic placement, a held tone, harmonic change, a pause, a chorus returning, or the anticipation of a moment may matter more than the words.

Our two linked objectives:

- **Teach AI to hear music as unfolding sound, like humans can.** Go beyond memorized lyrics, titles, artists, genres and superficial happy/sad tags. Identify actual time-localized sound, expression, expectation, change and structure. Include unfamiliar-language music, wordless vocals and instrumentals from the start.
- **Teach AI to understand the particular person it listens with, improving appropriately over time.** Through *consented* history, corrections, counterexamples and changing reactions, can it learn which details this person attends to, what they say those details mean, and when to remain uncertain or quiet?

We distinguish **auditory competence**, **understanding an individual's expressed experience**, and **having subjective feelings oneself**. They are not interchangeable. We should never claim an AI feels what a human feels because it produced touching words. Nor should we dismiss a person's experience just because its mechanism remains unknown.

### 4. Your mission: challenge how learning might work

Develop a *scientifically contestable*, computationally useful theory that joins musical pattern recognition to individual human meaning **without falsely collapsing either into the other**. We want separate claims about perception, interpretation, memory, interaction and model adaptation, with clear points where the AI must ask a human.

### 5. Questions from Sol — investigate these deeply

These questions are not a quiz with known answers. Group or challenge them; identify which distinctions actually matter. For the strongest ones, propose what an AI system could observe, what **only a listener** can report, and a disconfirming experiment.

**Sound and body.** Why can a pulse, syncopation, bass texture, breath or vocal strain affect humans before they can name the instrument or emotion? What role might entrainment, motor anticipation, proprioception, interoception or movement play? Can a model without a human body learn any useful functional analogue, and how would we test it without pretending it has bodily feelings?

**Time and prediction.** Does a musical event move someone because of the event itself, what preceded it, what they expect next, or what they remember from prior listens? How can we distinguish prediction-error effects from familiarity, repetition, dynamic intensity and personal association?

**Words versus voice.** What is preserved when a listener does not understand the lyrics? Can models learn timbre, phrasing and perceived expression without smuggling in translation, artist biographies or culturally learned clichés? What should remain unknown about the singer's intended emotion and the listener's response?

**Memory, biography and identity.** Why can a specific song act as a portal to a life period or a person even when the acoustic features are ordinary? How can an AI ask about personal meaning without extracting intimate stories or treating a moment as a permanent psychological profile? How would it recognize that the same person's response has changed?

**Culture and disagreement.** How much of musical emotion is shared or culturally learned? Can two people hear opposite emotional meanings in the same recording while both are right about their own experience? How should a model represent disagreement without erasing it or asserting a universal emotion label?

**The social moment.** What actually happens when one person shares a beloved song with another? Is the value in synchrony, joint attention, vulnerability, anticipation of the other's reaction, recognition, or something else? Can a co-listener add value through silence and a well-timed question rather than a constant stream of observations?

**Attention and pattern learning.** Some humans hear production textures, some notice harmony, some attend to a single voice, and others notice what is absent. Can an AI discover an individual's *attention signature* through few, voluntary examples and generalize it to unseen tracks? What is the most compelling falsifier of this hypothesis?

**Meaning beyond labels.** If somebody says “I can't explain why this destroys me,” is forcing them to choose sad/joyful/nostalgic a category error? What representational form would preserve ambiguity without becoming vague or unfalsifiable?

**The difference between resonance and flattery.** How might an AI deliver an emotionally persuasive reply despite never having heard the sound? How do we measure accurate connection separately from pleasant wording, mirroring and false intimacy?

**Learning over months.** What exactly would count as improved understanding *of the same human* across time: correct new-song predictions, better questions, fewer intrusive inferences, stable recognition of corrections, or something else? How do we isolate learning from memorization, dataset leakage and the human adapting to the model?

**Error as a teacher.** When a listener says “No, that's not why that section matters,” what should change—an acoustic annotation, a context-dependent personal hypothesis, or the conversational policy? What should remain unchanged? Can the model cite the correction's provenance and retract it later if needed?

**Research opportunity.** What important data already exists across music-information retrieval, cognitive neuroscience, psychology, cross-cultural music research and human-computer interaction? What critical dataset is *missing* if the goal is personal understanding? How can a public project collect non-sensitive, lawful, opt-in counterexamples without turning intimate songs into extractive data?

**The unknown.** Is there a part of musical communication we cannot operationalize yet? What observations would force us to abandon our favorite explanation and invent a better one?

Go beyond these prompts: bring **two research questions you believe Sol has missed**.

### 7. Evaluation discipline (Ω-ARENA)

Candidate procedures for how to listen, ask questions, store corrections, infer patterns or coordinate AI agents are **versioned challengers** rather than automatic persistent “learning.” Exploratory cases do not count as confirmation. Before a confirmatory experiment, freeze the candidate version, trigger, action, scope, comparator, outcome, quality and safety floor, cost ceiling, falsifiers, and future case-selection rule. A material modification means a new version. Compare in valid matched A/B, prospective parallel cases, or suitable ablation. Count tool/API calls, operator burden, context use, accessibility, latency and ongoing complexity—not just performance. If evidence is missing or contaminated, report **UNKNOWN**. Keep a record of losing ideas to avoid circular retests. Do not promote a method based on emotional agreement, AI-to-AI consensus, or an unblinded example.
