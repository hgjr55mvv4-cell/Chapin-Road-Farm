# Hearing music with the brain — evidence map
Reviewed October 22, 2026. This is a literature synthesis, **not** independent reanalysis of scans.

## Modalities matter

**fMRI:** blood-oxygen-level-dependent signals; indirect, statistical indicators of neural activity. **PET:** radiotracers for blood-flow or chemical measurements; the foundational 2001 music chills experiment was PET, not fMRI. **fNIRS:** surface cortical hemodynamics, not whole-brain fMRI. None directly reads private experience.

| Study and link | Modality | Actual finding and activation/network | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Blood & Zatorre, 2001, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.191355898) | **PET** | Self-selected chill-inducing music correlated with changes of blood flow in ventral striatum, midbrain, amygdala, orbitofrontal/prefrontal networks; some correlations positive, others negative | PET is not MRI; correlations do not read individual memory |
| [Janata, 2009, Cerebral Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhp008) | **fMRI** | Dorsal medial prefrontal cortex tracked how autobiographically significant childhood-era songs were | A relationship to memories, not the precise contents of a memory |
| [Salimpoor et al., 2011, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2726) | **PET + fMRI** | PET evidence for striatal dopamine release with peak musical pleasure; fMRI distinguished **caudate** during anticipation from **nucleus accumbens** at peak pleasure | PET and BOLD measure different things; selected listeners and stimuli |
| [Salimpoor et al., 2013, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1231059) | **fMRI** | Nucleus accumbens activity and connectivity with auditory cortex, amygdala and ventromedial prefrontal regions were related to reward value for newly heard music | Willingness-to-pay proxy differs from beloved personal songs |
| [Wallmark et al., 2018, Frontiers](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2018.00066) | **fMRI** | Empathy traits correlated with sensorimotor responses to timbre, and prefrontal/reward-related responses to excerpts, especially familiar music | Correlational; individual differences matter |
| [Frühholz et al., 2024, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2316306121) | **fMRI, n=27** | Adaptively played live piano with amygdala neurofeedback yielded stronger amygdala response than prerecorded performance, along with wider emotional network engagement | Live and recorded music were acoustically different; not an isolated social-presence test |
| [Jalon et al., 2026, Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76452-0) | **fMRI, n=20** | Excerpt-specific representations across imagined music narratives and spoken reports in **default mode, language and association** cortices | Prompted imagination; 2 excerpts excluded; not proof of identical experience |
| [Curzel et al., 2026, Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2026.02.012) | **fNIRS, 34 friend pairs** | Together-versus-alone listening increased similarity of continuous pleasure ratings and interpersonal neural synchrony; didn't universally increase pleasure | fNIRS, not MRI; synchrony ≠ shared consciousness |

## Brain regions: what may matter when sharing a song

- Auditory cortical networks: structure of sound and coupling with value.
- Caudate: expectation and the building anticipation before powerful sections (2011).
- Nucleus accumbens: peak pleasure and valuation in tested paradigms (2011, 2013).
- Amygdala: emotional salience, not a simplistic 'fear-only' button (2024).
- Dorsal medial prefrontal cortex: personal autobiographical salience (2009).
- Default mode and association cortex: constructions of imagined meaning and narrative (2026).

**Interpretation:** A useful co-listener should track specific musical moments, invitation to personal memory, interpretation, and differences between listeners. This is a research hypothesis, not an observed neural ability of any AI.
