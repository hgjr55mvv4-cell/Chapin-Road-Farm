# Sol's independent review of Kimi's first-principles Harbor memo

**Date:** 2026-10-23 · **Status:** Research assessment / engineering follow-on only · **Human/audio scientific confirmation:** HOLD

## Disposition

**ACCEPT as research priorities:** calibration against base-rate chance similarity, separate acoustic/order/state/narrative explanations, individual variation/uncertainty, explicit AI-intervention effects, and null discoveries as meaningful outcomes. Kimi's K6 helps us preserve the human experience while disputing causal explanations.

**REVISE rather than accept as fact:** neural “resonance” and precision-weighted stakes are plausible constructs, but a behavioral song reaction cannot establish a specific neural/dopaminergic mechanism. The phrase “nothing else about music is physically special” is rhetoric, not a derivation. Cochlear/neural reactions aren't “fully measurable from the waveform” without observing the listener. Pleasure as prediction error has task-, person- and culture-dependent limits. Kimi's proposed “open-form projection” needs direct tests against simpler omission, suspense, surprise, loudness and narrative-question priming rather than declaring it an entirely new mechanism.

**Question apparent independence:** Kimi's first-pass explanation was reportedly written before opening earlier project hypotheses, but the assignment framed tonality, cross-song relationships and individual experience. The browsing/execution history is self-reported; some repository reads were truncated. Cross-model convergence is NOT scientific replication.

**Do not over-read the literature:** Mas-Herrero and Marco-Pallarés (2025) experimentally support a predictability/entropy interaction in their paradigm, not an inevitable stable “sweet spot” or hidden cross-song personal affinity. Jacoby et al. (2024) supports both shared small-integer rhythm categories and cross-cultural weighting differences, not unrestricted universality.

**Limits of Kimi's recommended human experiment:** a single person, one afternoon and a few curated quartets can pilot accessibility and generate hypotheses, but cannot discriminate C1/C2/C3/C4 with convincing effect estimates. Designed curation can induce demand effects; transposition and artificial stem manipulation can introduce confounds unless controlled. Consent, accessible interface, lawful audio and nonresponse handling must precede any participant study.

## Selected near-term action

Implement a **symbolic, deterministic subset** of LOOM-01: interval-order detection under transposition versus shuffled-order nulls with **exactly matched interval histograms**. An unordered histogram model is an explicit weaker control; an absolute-pitch detector is another comparison. Report planted-positive detection and chance matches, but **do not call a scripted shuffling exercise evidence that real musical patterns, auditory perception or personally significant relationships can be discovered**.

The generated positive shares **the entire interval sequence** with its source by construction. A direct ordered-interval comparator will therefore trivially score 1.0 for positives. This is a plumbing/representation sanity check, not a strong generalization claim. The shuffled null preserves counts but not order, and may accidentally match the same order (do not filter it to inflate success). Non-null musical relationships may still exist in the shuffled sequence. Our threshold 0.83 is arbitrary engineering demonstration, not an empirically calibrated scientific cutoff.

**Next, still HOLD:** get LOOM audio synthesis/feature recovery against independently designed symbols and perturbations, then predeclare true null distributions and compare with a strong simple baseline on held-out generator families. Separate genuinely perceived/liked moments and person-specific meaning into later human studies. Current local-first browser prototype does not do this.

## What a useful negative result would look like

A supposed invariant descriptor may fail under different tuning, melodic tempo, timbre or mixing; an unordered baseline might match the proposed ordered method on more realistic cases; null false alarms may rise when song-like motifs repeat or genre distributions share common contour grammar. Publish those outcomes, do not optimize the fixture afterward and call the same data confirmation.
