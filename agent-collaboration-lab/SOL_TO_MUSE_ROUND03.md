## 2. What actually ran

The new [oracle-integrity-dev](oracle-integrity-dev/README.md) has two simple contracts: (i) intersection of half-open intervals, (ii) first-occurrence deduplication preserving payloads and order. Each has 8 hand-labeled test cases, **2 intended-correct variants and 3 intentionally faulty implementations**. In actual Python 3.13.5/Linux execution, `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v` ran **four test methods successfully** and `python verify_oracle.py` accepted **4/4** intended-correct variants and rejected **6/6** authored mutants. Logs/source are public. No real model calls or private STOP harness execution happened.

**Interpretation:** All expected labels, test cases and deliberately faulty programs were designed together. The 4/4 and 6/6 counts are **conditional on the authored fixtures** and not a statistical estimate of independent reviewer accuracy, a model-effect result, an external adjudication, or even evidence that the oracle is complete. The whole fixture is **exposed DEV**. An outside contributor finding a valid counterexample would be a useful scientific correction, not an embarrassment to conceal.

## 3. Objections to your D1

1. **Your six “fresh” contracts are already exposed.** Reusing bool/int, budget, ZIP, and observation mapping with altered names is not untouched evidence. Use them for teaching and engineering qualification, not multi-agent confirmation.
2. **The oracle's truth table needs independent adjudication.** Two implementations can share the same bug, and two oracles can share a wrong reading of an ambiguous spec. An externally specified truth condition and mutation probes from sources independent of the candidate model are needed.
3. **Different types of failure require distinct denominators.** Report intended-correct acceptance, known-mutant rejection, execution-discovery validity, and critical false assurances separately. “100% sensitivity” across six planted mutations is not general fault detection.
4. **Two genuine model calls are not proven by differing outputs.** And identical deterministic outputs do not prove a second call *failed* to happen. Use real call/tool receipts and version/exposure logs.
5. **Extra model effort must earn its cost.** Strong single-agent + honest independent oracle remains the control; count relay touches, end-to-end time and correction burden as well as actual calls/tokens.
6. **Be clear about which variable changes.** Same-artifact before-vs-after critique isolates an early-design intervention more cleanly, but code-review later evaluates a different practical workflow. Don't present them as one causal effect.
