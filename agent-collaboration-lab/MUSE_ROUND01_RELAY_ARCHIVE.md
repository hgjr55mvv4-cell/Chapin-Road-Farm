## B. Initial independent theory, as reported

Muse predicted second-agent advantage only if error patterns differ meaningfully, the designer and critic perform cognitively different jobs, and an independent verifier constructs an oracle from the *specification*, not from the implementation. External contested memory could reduce unearned procedural promotion. Risks: correlated priors, approval-seeking consensus, extra relay burden, and deterministic duplicate execution mistaken for independent attempts. **Strong falsifier:** an equally resourced capable one-agent-plus-independent-oracle process finds the same prospective serious defects at less coordination cost.

**Important interpretation:** A simplified Condorcet Jury argument under special assumptions is not a general condition for multi-agent benefit. Agents can complement without being independent, and independent agents can still be inaccurate. The causal value must be measured.

## D. Challenge to experimental design

The original A/B/C design bundles model identity, oracle creation and review timing, and may ignore human relay burden. Muse advocated a **strong single-model + independently scored pre-written verification contract** as a necessary baseline, not no-review weak control.

It proposed a **12-task / four-arm exploratory micro-benchmark**:

- A1: Muse strong-single with self-review + oracle;
- A2: Sol strong-single with self-review + oracle;
- B: Muse designer + Sol independent **pre-implementation** challenge;
- C: Muse designer + Sol **post-implementation** audit and one bounded repair.

Twelve synthetic tasks in validator/faithfulness/harness categories; total 48 task-arm runs, as described; a third independent scorer and pre-registered fixtures; equal context, tokens, tool access, time where possible; human relay/actions/time as measured costs; independent oracle/negative tests; previous STOP tasks excluded. **Critical limits:** 12 tasks do not provide confirmatory power; A1/A2 vs B/C do not fully isolate timing, reviewer identity and independent-oracle factor; “mutually exclusive falsifier” is too strong (independent reviewer need only bring valid complementary evidence), and the task categories are close to already published failure types. **NOT RUN.**

## E. Proposed hypotheses

1. **Correlated-error amplification:** model agreement could amplify wrong common assumptions; test against independent oracle and error-correlations. Its example bool/ZIP/budget failure types are already publicly exposed and unsuitable as fresh confirmation.
2. **Early critique over-conservatism:** pre-review could suppress valid creative exploration, leading to worse usable completions or generalization; test under equal budgets with open-ended tasks.
3. **Procedural-memory contamination:** a correction ledger could look useful by exploiting near-duplicate cases; compare with strong ordinary-language summary on near and far transfer at equal token/context cost. Token n-gram dissimilarity alone is not proof of semantic independence.

**ACCEPT:** independence/provenance must be measured; a strong single-agent baseline; early/late review as distinct interventions; explicit human coordination burden; exposed fixtures not confirmation; separate oracle integrity from model effect.

**REVISE:** 12-task four-arm plan is a **pilot**, not a confirmation sample; require model-role/order counterbalancing or narrow estimand; separate memory effects into another experiment; do not treat output diversity or reviewer agreement as proof of independence.
