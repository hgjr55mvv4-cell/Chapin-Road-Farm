## 1. Mission and ambition
The Listening Room seeks a useful human–AI co-listener that learns from what someone says about music, suggests songs attuned to them and accepts corrections. Muse's Round 2 Quick Moment and Constellation Playlist concept invites people to connect 3–7 songs that *belong together for them now*, even across genres. Grok warned that apparent 'hidden musical patterns' may be generated by prompts, familiarity, selection bias or persuasive explanations. DeepSeek proposed a synthetic schema and small Python harness; Claude's initial metadata-only app remains interrupted.

**Build a working, original Constellation Lab v0.1**, separate from the unfinished app. Make it possible to inspect entirely fictional meaningful-song sets, reject unsupported acoustic claims, record corrections and permission boundaries, and compare genuinely competitive preference/meaning hypotheses. We want functioning code, reproducible evidence and **two fresh ideas** that can be falsified, not a long concept paper.

## 3. Repair the concrete DeepSeek weaknesses
The independent transcription of DeepSeek's Python harness printed **14/14**, whereas DeepSeek's expected output said **15/15**. Adversarial inspection found:
- Unrecognized recording identity strings were accepted.
- Caller-set 'verified' identity/version and 'authorized_audio=True' admitted an audio observation **without any actual measurement evidence**.
- An association could point to a non-existent song.
- B-structured's empty association object was not used by its ranker; no actual representation ablation.
- Corrections targeting missing events were silently ignored.
- 'Declined' could retain text; a refusal test just searched for the word 'personality'.

Independently reproduce or reject these findings. No new evidence from an unverified source may be assumed. **Stage 0 must always fail closed on acoustic/event/timestamp claims, regardless of self-asserted flags**; Stage 1 is future-only, requiring an independently audited authorized-audio pipeline.

## 4. Build an executable minimal core
Prefer Python 3 standard library, JSON and optionally a small offline HTML/CSS/vanilla JS viewer. Use no servers, account credentials, music-streaming access, paid APIs, telemetry or network fetches.

**A. Candidate schema and validator.** Separate synthetic-only v0.2-DEV; do not modify v0.1. Enforce unique identifiers and playlist references, valid reason_status (provided/declined/unknown/not_asked), recording version/identity ambiguity, correction and supersession target integrity, temporal ordering/cycle checks, avoidance targets and user-authorized persistence. Keep public-fixture constants: synthetic=true; research_use=false; public_release=false; model_training=false. Reject malformed/hostile input and unsupported claim types before state mutation. Test duplicate JSON keys if the parser can detect them.

**B. Private state, corrections and deletion simulator.** Show session-only defaults, opt-in local preferences, consent withdrawal, corrections and changed preferences, deterministic never-recommend veto, export, and actual removal of simulated personal content. Append-only update history is not a license to retain content the listener deleted. Declining to explain must not create any inferred emotional/personality label or persistent refusal profile.

**C. Evidence-bound claim gate.** At Stage 0 admit only attributed HUMAN_REPORT, sourced TRACK_METADATA, clearly hypothetical SYSTEM_HYPOTHESIS and UNKNOWN, checking actual supporting fields. No actual audio observation is possible, even if a field claims audio is authorized. Reject sentences such as 'guitar starts at 2:14', 'these all build in the same way', 'your songs share an emotional architecture' absent relevant evidence. Specify an external future Stage 1 proof receipt (recording version, legal access, observation method, ground truth, calibration and tolerance); do not implement an easily forged Stage 1 unlock.

**D. Strong baselines and partitions.** Implement small deterministic ranking over a fixed synthetic catalog:
- A0: supported metadata plus explicit preferences, without a question.
- A1: strong preference model including ordinary musical descriptors, current purpose, dislikes, set cohesion and (in future UI) one optional question.
- B-info: A1 plus separately volunteered associations. Information is *unequal*—record that honestly.
- B-flat vs B-structured: *identical frozen raw data and resources*; compare representations only if the structured method actually changes model behavior. If it cannot, record NOT EVALUABLE rather than invent improvement.

Audit leaks: A1 cannot inadvertently receive association-derived text, vectors, Boolean statuses or semantic proxies for a comparison that denies them. Explicit avoidance excludes tracks before ranking, always. A synthetic demonstration does not prove listener preference.

**E. Constellation explorer.** Create a readable local/offline viewer with 3–7 invented tracks from very different genres, user-authored fictional comments, playlist connection reports, candidate recommendations, correction control and visible limitations. Display differences between listener testimony, metadata and hypothesis. Clearly label **audio not accessed**, **recording unverified** and **commonality UNKNOWN**. Mobile layout, keyboard focus, reduced-motion behavior and screen-reader labels should be designed/tested where tools permit.

**F. Contribution-sharing preview (not an intake).** People may choose private-only, protected research contribution, open sharing of some or **all selected** playlist entries, attribution display preference (named/pseudonymous/no-display-name), and separate model-training consent. Show a preview and realistic public permanence warnings. Privacy-default must not mean paternalistic private-only. Public GitHub remains code/synthetic fixtures only. No real user submission controls should transmit data.
