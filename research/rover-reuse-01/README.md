# ROVER-REUSE-01 — A second life for mobility equipment

**Status: OPEN RESEARCH / DESIGN ONLY · $0 additional spending · NO HARDWARE PROCUREMENT · NO AUTONOMOUS DRIVING · NO LASER OR OTHER TREATMENT**

This is a small public **green-teaming collaboration** between Chapin Road Farm (Hampden, Massachusetts), Brian's brother Jimmy (Indiana), Jimmy's Claude agent, Sol, and an independently tasked Muse agent. Jimmy suggested that a used mobility scooter might provide an affordable and already-integrated electric drive platform for a sensing rover—motors, batteries, wheels and brakes—rather than converting a remote-controlled mower. The idea has not yet been field-qualified, purchased, or built. The project values **curiosity, shared learning, accessibility and human relationships**, not merely time saved.

> **Our question:** Could a responsible, low-cost, repairable, repurposed mobility platform carry farm **sensors and cameras** over suitable paths, without requiring a risky scratch-built drivetrain—and what evidence would make us reject the idea?

## Distinguish three candidate bases

| Candidate | Initial appeal | Key unknown |
|---|---|---|
| **Four-wheel mobility scooter** | Existing integrated drive, charging and brakes, sometimes available used | Steering actuator/interface, low clearance, slope/traction, reverse behavior, charger health |
| **Powered wheelchair base** | Often two independently driven wheels and compact turning radius | Proprietary controls, casters in soil, weatherproofing, stability, safe independent brake/control |
| **Remote-controlled mower / purpose-built rover** | May be rugged or better suited to grass and obstacles | Cost, treatment hardware risk, weight, access to control interface, maintainability |

Do not assume availability, price or licensing from a representative listing. Compare **model-specific** evidence, not product category stereotypes.

## Why this is more than a cheap-chassis exercise

This is also an experiment in **cross-distance human–AI collaboration**. One family member noticed an overlooked reuse option; an AI collaborator can independently investigate it; another can challenge assumptions; and the farm can decide whether anything is worth testing. Preserve attribution, corrections, negative results, and the things people learned. Never imply that saving dollars or time is the sole measure of value.

**Ecological principle:** observe and understand first. Sensor missions must not casually disturb farm wildlife, insects, soil, crops or visitors. No organism is labeled a pest merely because it is unfamiliar.

## A credible research outcome could be NO-GO

If steering/stop/braking independence, battery condition, outdoor traction, ground clearance, maneuverability or serviceability cannot be evidenced, **decline the platform**. A useful failure analysis is a contribution.

Existing rover and laser-weeding planning is **not replaced** by this idea. The separate sensor/pointing work can continue; hazardous laser treatment and automatic field actuation remain **HOLD**.
